The 1.42 MeV mass discrepancy for o⁺ (Δ⁺) — and analogous offsets
of 0.85-1.58 MeV for the other three Δ ground states — must
therefore come from a P=3 specific term in φ that:
  - Is non-zero for P=3 (Δ family)
  - Is zero (or much smaller) for P ∈ {0, 1, 2}
  - We currently do not compute

Per-particle discrepancies (mass = Heim − ours):
  o⁺⁺ (q=+2):  Δm = +1.58 MeV  (Δbracket = +680)
  o⁺  (q=+1):  Δm = +1.42 MeV  (Δbracket = +611)
  o⁰  (q= 0):  Δm = +0.85 MeV  (Δbracket = +366)

The discrepancy varies with q but in a non-monotonic way.  This
rules out a simple q-linear missing term.  A missing term involving
both P=3 and some q-dependent factor (e.g. (1-q²)(1+q²/k)) is
the closest functional fit.

discrepancy (W₀ off by 6.85 → propagates to mass via greedy +
φ).  But for o⁺ and o⁻ where W₀ matches Heim's to 10⁻⁴ and
(n,m,p,σ) matches Heim's published values exactly, the
discrepancy is purely in φ.
